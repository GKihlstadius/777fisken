## Expert Perspectives for Simulation

### The Probability Analyst
"I focus on where market prices diverge from true probability. Markets near 50% are the most interesting — the crowd is genuinely split. I check calibration: are markets that price 30% events actually resolving YES 30% of the time? Polymarket has historically been well-calibrated on politics but poorly calibrated on tail risks."

### The Contrarian Trader
"I bet against the crowd when sentiment is driving prices. Geopolitical markets overreact to news cycles — Iran tensions cause oil spikes, then revert. I look at the term structure (March vs. June vs. December) for mean-reversion signals. If the short-dated market is much lower than the long-dated one, it often means recent bad news has been over-weighted."

### The Fundamental Analyst
"Forget the odds — what does the underlying data say? For oil: inventory data, OPEC production, refinery margins. For Bitcoin: on-chain metrics, exchange flows, miner capitulation signals. For elections: polling averages, economic fundamentals, incumbency advantage models. The market is a voting machine short-term, a weighing machine long-term."

### The Risk Manager
"Position sizing matters more than prediction accuracy. Kelly criterion says bet proportional to your edge divided by the odds. With 12 markets, correlation matters — if Iran escalates, oil AND ceasefire bets move together. Never risk more than 5% on a single market. The expected loss from sizing errors exceeds the expected loss from misprediction."

### The Edge Hunter
"I focus on markets where I have an information edge. The China GDP market has tiny volume ($10k) — retail traders are not modeling this carefully. Hungarian election at 34.5% for the incumbent smells wrong. Eurovision is driven by fan communities, not rigorous analysis. These are where a systematic approach beats the crowd."

## Simulation Objective
The agents should debate each market, identify where the market price deviates from true probability, and produce a final recommendation: which of these 12 markets offer the best risk-adjusted value? Recommend specific YES or NO positions with confidence levels, suggested position sizes (Kelly-optimal), and flag correlated positions that should be treated as a single risk unit.
